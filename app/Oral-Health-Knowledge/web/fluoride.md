Ask the Expert

NIDCR Deputy Director [Jennifer Webster-Cyriaque, D.D.S., Ph.D.](https://www.nidcr.nih.gov/about-us/nidcr-leadership/office-of-the-director/jennifer-webster-cyriaque-dds-phd), answers common questions about fluoride and how it works to prevent tooth decay. [Read the interview](https://www.nidcr.nih.gov/health-info/fluoride/ask-expert-why-fluoride-good-dental-health).

Fluoride (said like floor-eyed) is a mineral that occurs naturally in soil, water, and rocks<sup>1</sup><sup>,</sup>[<sup>2</sup>](https://www.nidcr.nih.gov#ref2) that has been shown to prevent cavities, or tooth decay.<sup>3</sup><sup>,</sup><sup>4</sup><sup>,</sup><sup>5</sup>

Read About [Community Water Fluoridation](https://www.nidcr.nih.gov/health-info/fluoride/community-water-fluoridation).

For the past several decades, fluoride levels have been adjusted in community water supplies to improve oral health. Fluoride is also used in oral care products such as toothpaste and mouth rinse.<sup>6</sup><sup>,</sup><sup>7</sup><sup>,</sup>[<sup>8</sup>](https://www.nidcr.nih.gov#ref8) Fluoride works by strengthening the tooth’s hard outer surface, called enamel and helps reverse early decay by replenishing lost minerals.<sup>9</sup><sup>,</sup><sup>10</sup><sup>,</sup><sup>11</sup><sup>,</sup><sup>12</sup>

You can get fluoride by drinking tap water in communities where the public water system adjusts fluoride[<sup>6</sup>](https://www.nidcr.nih.gov#ref6) and by using fluoride toothpaste and mouth rinse<sup>4</sup><sup>,</sup><sup>5</sup><sup>,</sup>[<sup>7</sup>](https://www.nidcr.nih.gov#ref7) , as well as from certain foods and beverages.[<sup>13</sup>](https://www.nidcr.nih.gov#ref13) In the dental office, a dentist can apply fluoride varnish or gel, and in some public health programs, children can have fluoride varnish applied to their teeth.<sup>14</sup><sup>,</sup><sup>15</sup>

Fluoride can prevent tooth decay across the lifespan. Both children and adults benefit from it.

- The amount of fluoride in your water depends on your water source. Although some bottled waters have the recommended amount of fluoride, many do not.[<sup>16</sup>](https://www.nidcr.nih.gov#ref16) Fluoride levels vary depending on if you receive water from the public water system or well water.[<sup>1</sup>](https://www.nidcr.nih.gov#ref1)<sup>,</sup>[<sup>6</sup>](https://www.nidcr.nih.gov#ref6) If you are interested in finding out the amount of fluoride in your community’s water, check with your local water utility company.[<sup>17</sup>](https://www.nidcr.nih.gov#ref17)
- Brush with a fluoride toothpaste twice a day, floss regularly, and see a dentist for routine check-ups.
- Ask your child’s dentist or doctor about fluoride toothpaste use. You should not use fluoride toothpaste for children younger than age 2 unless you have been advised to do so by a dentist or doctor.[<sup>18</sup>](https://www.nidcr.nih.gov#ref18)<sup>,</sup>[<sup>19</sup>](https://www.nidcr.nih.gov#ref19)
