Sores (blisters and ulcers) on the tongue can be caused by:

- Irritation from certain foods or chemicals
- [Oral herpes simplex virus infection](https://www.msdmanuals.com/home/infections/herpesvirus-infections/herpes-simplex-virus-hsv-infections#v788760)
- Canker sores ( [recurrent aphthous stomatitis](https://www.msdmanuals.com/home/mouth-and-dental-disorders/symptoms-of-oral-and-dental-disorders/recurrent-aphthous-stomatitis) )
- [Tuberculosis](https://www.msdmanuals.com/home/infections/tuberculosis-and-related-infections/tuberculosis-tb)
- Bacterial infections
- [Early-stage syphilis](https://www.msdmanuals.com/home/infections/sexually-transmitted-infections-stis/syphilis#v790361)
- [Allergies or other immune system disorders](https://www.msdmanuals.com/home/immune-disorders/allergic-reactions-and-other-hypersensitivity-disorders/overview-of-allergic-reactions)
- [Oral cancer](https://www.msdmanuals.com/home/mouth-and-dental-disorders/symptoms-of-oral-and-dental-disorders/mouth-growths#v6528508)
- Injury from biting the tongue

The side of the tongue may enlarge slightly into a space left by a missing teeth. This is usually harmless. Although small bumps on both sides of the tongue are usually harmless, a bump on only one side should be examined to see if it is cancerous.

Erythroplakia and Squamous Cell Carcinoma

Erythroplakia is a general term for red, flat, or worn-away velvet-looking sores that develop in the mouth. In this image, a squamous cell carcinoma is surrounded by a margin of erythroplakia.

Unexplained red or white areas, sores, or lumps (particularly when hard) on the tongue—especially if painless—may be signs of cancer and should be examined by a doctor or dentist. Most [oral cancers](https://www.msdmanuals.com/home/cancer/mouth-nose-and-throat-cancers/mouth-and-throat-cancer) grow on the sides of the tongue or on the floor of the mouth. Cancer almost never appears on the top of the tongue, except when the cancer occurs after untreated syphilis.
