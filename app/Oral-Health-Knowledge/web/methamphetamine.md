Methamphetamine can be smoked, snorted, injected, or taken orally.<sup>4, 5</sup> Typically, it is a bitter-tasting powder that readily dissolves in water or alcohol.<sup>3</sup> Another common form of the drug is a clear, chunky crystal. This is the form known as “ice” or “crystal meth” and it is smoked in a manner similar to crack cocaine.<sup>3</sup> Methamphetamine can also be in the form of small, brightly colored tablets, sometimes combined with caffeine; these pills are often called by their Thai name, yaba.<sup>3, 4</sup>

Methamphetamine stimulates release and blocks reuptake of neurotransmitters called monoamines (dopamine, norepinephrine and serotonin) in the brain.<sup>3</sup> Several areas of the brain are affected: the nucleus accumbens, prefrontal cortex, and striatum.<sup>3, 6</sup>

By altering the levels of neurotransmitters in the brain, including dopamine, methamphetamine causes feelings of pleasure and euphoria.<sup>3,7</sup> Methamphetamine is a neurotoxin and potent stimulant, which can also cause cerebral edema and hemorrhage, paranoia and hallucinations.<sup>3</sup> Short-term effects include vomiting, insomnia, hyperactivity, decreased appetite, increased respiration and tremors.<sup>2, 5</sup> Long-term effects can include psychological addiction, stroke, violent behavior, anxiety, confusion, paranoia, auditory hallucination, mood disturbances, and delusions.<sup>2, 5</sup> Methamphetamine use can eventually cause depletion of monoamines in the brain, which can have an effect on learning.<sup>3, 8, 9</sup>

Systemic effects of methamphetamine may include an increase in blood pressure and rapid heartbeat, due to cardiac stimulation, especially with high doses.<sup>2, 5</sup> In addition, methamphetamine may produce cardiac arrhythmias.<sup>5</sup> Other systemic effects include: shortness of breath, hyperthermia, nausea, vomiting and diarrhea.<sup>2, 5</sup>
