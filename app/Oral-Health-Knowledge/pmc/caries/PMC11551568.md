# Frontiers of Global Research Trend on Root Caries: A Bibliometric Analysis

**Authors:** Grace Yuchan Xu, Irene Shuping Zhao, Christie Ying Kei Lung, Iris Xiaoxue Yin, Edward Chin Man Lo, Chun Hung Chu

## Objective

This study aimed to examine the global research trend and frontiers in the field of root caries.

## Methods

Two independent investigators searched the Web of Science Core Collection to include journal articles published on root caries from 1994 to 2023. They examined citation trends of the selected publications and performed bibliometric analysis using VOSviewer. Cooccurrence and cocitation analysis were used to calculate the burst strength of the most cited keywords and the most cited publications.

## Results

This study included 1144 publications (1004 scientific research articles and 140 review articles) for bibliometric analysis. The number of publications from 2018 to 2023 was 404, accounting for 35% (404/1144) over the last 30 years. The annual citation count showed a gradual upward trend with a surge from 2018. The cooccurrence analysis classified the publications into four clusters which were in vitro studies, prevention, microbiology, and epidemiology. Cocitation analysis revealed silver diamine fluoride, systematic review, and randomized clinical trial were the three main keywords; and their citation burst strength (period) were 17.2 (2017-2023), 9.4 (2015-2023), and 6.9 (2018-2023), respectively. Noninvasive treatment of root caries (narrative review), aetiology of root caries (narrative review), and use of silver diamine fluoride in arresting root caries (clinical trial) were the topics (publication type) of the three most cited publications; and their burst strength (period) were 8.9 (2017-2020), 5.9 (2017-2021), and 4.7 (2015-2018), respectively.

## Conclusion

This study highlights the recent growing research interest in root caries, particularly on its microbiology, prevention, and the use of silver diamine fluoride.

## Introduction

Dental caries can be classified as root caries or coronal caries based on the location. Normally, the tooth root is protected and covered by periodontal tissue. However, with recession of periodontal tissue, cementum or dentine will be exposed to the oral cavity. 1 Root caries will develop in the presence of cariogenic biofilm and fermentable carbohydrates over an extended period of time. 2 Caries progression on the surface of root dentine occurs approximately twice as fast as on coronal enamel surfaces. 3 Dentine and cementum have lower mineral contents than enamel. 4 The demineralization threshold pH value for dentin (6.2-6.4) is higher compared to enamel (5.5). 5 Moreover, the collagenases can degrade dentine collagens during the process of demineralization. 6

The prevalence and incidence of root caries are increasing due to the increasing ageing population and the retention of natural teeth in older adults. 7 , 8 The World Health Organization anticipates that the number of adults aged 65 and above will double by 2050. 9 Studies have shown that the incidence of root-surface caries correlates with advancing age, gender, residential environment, and clasp tooth. 10 , 11 With ageing, factors like gingival recession and decreased saliva production contribute to a higher risk of root caries development. If left untreated, the root caries may lead to severe pain and affect wellness. Despite advancements in healthcare and treatment, the prevalence of untreated root caries in older adults remains high since 1990. 12

Restoring root caries is challenging due to difficulty in moisture control and maintaining marginal integrity, leading to recurrent lesions. 13 In addition, access to proximal carious lesions is difficult and it often requires extensive removal of sound tooth tissues. Therefore, some dentists offer minimally invasive therapy instead of the traditional ‘drilling and filling’ procedures. However, the success of restoration for root caries is compromised because of the close proximity of the gingival margin and difficulties associated with dentin bonding. 14 Moreover, due to limitations in their functional capability, older patients frequently encounter restricted treatment ability, particularly for those who reside in long-term care facilities.

Root caries is becoming a concern because of its rapid progression, increased incidence, and unsatisfactory treatment. Researchers are looking for various strategies for root caries management. Understanding the global research trends on root caries can help identify the most effective measures and treatments for patients. Studying global research trends on root caries allows for the identification of existing knowledge gaps and areas warranting future exploration. This can ultimately improve treatment outcomes and overall oral health. Additionally, exploring global research trends on root caries facilitates collaborative efforts among researchers and institutions working on similar topics, fostering increased efficiency in research outcomes.

Bibliometric analysis, a computer-assisted scientific review methodology, enables the visualization and analysis of publications on root caries. It facilitates the identification of core research keywords and their interrelationships. 15 It also helps identify key authors and collaborations, fostering interdisciplinary collaboration and knowledge exchange. By analysing citation patterns, bibliometric analysis can identify highly cited papers, influential authors, and leading journals in the field of root caries. This information helps researchers understand the impact and importance of specific research outputs. Bibliometric analysis has been used extensively in dentistry to analyse research trends. 16 , 17 The objective of this study is to examine the global research trends and frontiers of studies on root caries from 1994 to 2023 using bibliometric analysis.

## Methods

Two independent researchers conducted a systematic search in January 2024 using the Web of Science Core Collection (WoSCC) to obtain global scientific publications on root caries published from 1994-01-01 to 2023-12-31. Figure 1 provides details about the search strategy and selection procedure. VOSviewer v.1.6.17 (Leiden University, Leiden, Netherlands) was used to conduct the bibliometric analysis for this study. The meeting abstract, proceeding paper, editorial material, and letter were not included. Although removal of meeting abstracts from the inclusion criteria is a limitation, the meeting abstracts typically present preliminary findings, which may have not undergone a vigorous peer-review process. This could result in less reliable or incomplete data being included in the analysis. In addition, abstracts of good-quality studies are often published in journals afterwards. Including both the abstract and the article in the analysis may result in duplication and overrepresentation of certain studies. By focusing on peer-reviewed and full-length articles, researchers aim to improve the quality, reliability, and generalizability of the findings in their bibliometric analysis. Two researchers independently examined abstracts and titles and excluding irrelevant articles. The researchers consulted a third investigator to address their different opinions on whether to include or exclude certain publications. The researchers subsequently retrieved the full transcripts for further analysis. The bar charts and line charts presented the trends in annual publication and citation counts separately. A figure depicted the cooccurrence relation among the top 20 countries with the highest number of publications. Another figure displayed the top 10 journals with the most citations. Subsequent analyses included cooccurrence and cocitation analysis of references and keywords. Burst detection of keywords was employed to identify those exhibiting a rapid increase in frequency within a short period, indicating the current research hotspots. ## Results

Researchers identified 1310 root caries-related publications between 1994 and 2023 in the WoSCC database. A total of 178 references were excluded, including meeting abstract ( n = 110), proceeding paper ( n = 42), editorial material ( n = 19), and letter ( n = 7). Only the journal articles undergoing peer review were included. A total of 1144 publications were ultimately examined to explore global research interest in root caries ( Figure 1 ).

Over the past three decades, annual citation counts have significantly increased. The most notable growth of citations occurs from n = 1927 in 2018 to n = 3098 in 2021. It represents an impressive surge of 1.6 times within a 3-year period. Furthermore, the annual publication frequency has witnessed a sharp rise with the past 5 years contributing 35% of the total publications. ## Discussion

This article reviews the trends and hotspots of root caries research in the past three decades. Both the annual citation frequency and publications in the field of root caries have had a significant increase. Root caries is prevalent in elderly people due to a variety of reasons including gum recession, denture wear, poor oral hygiene, and age-related changes in saliva composition. An exploration of the worldwide research landscape on root caries equips researchers with in-depth understanding of the effectiveness of different management strategies. This focus on root caries in older adults is in line with broader efforts to promote healthy ageing and enhance longevity. Despite ranking fourth in terms of publication output, Japan has the highest number of citations among all countries. The top three journals with most citations are Journal of Dental Research, Caries Research , and Journal of Dentistry . Keyword analysis identified four main clusters of research, which are in vitro studies, prevention, microbiology, and epidemiology. It is worth noting that ‘silver ammonium fluoride’ has been a popular keyword in recent years.

The result of this review aligns with the widespread use of SDF in randomized clinical trials. 23 , 28 , 29 , 30 , 31 , 32 , 33 SDF is an alkaline solution comprising fluoride and silver. When faced with mobility limits, older adults would choose SDF to maintain teeth as a more feasible therapy. 34 A systematic review found that SDF reduced increment of root caries by more than 25% compared to control group. 35 The annual application of SDF significantly reduced the incidence of root caries in older adults by 41% at the 2-year follow-up. 35 SDF exhibits antibacterial properties that hinder the growth of cariogenic bacteria and reduce the complexity and viability of microcolonies within biofilms on demineralized root dentin. 36 SDF promotes remineralization as well as prevents demineralization, which helps to harden the infected tooth structure. SDF suppresses collagen breakdown by inactivating collagenases. 37 SDF has been reported to be effective in managing both root surface caries and coronal caries in permanent and primary dentitions. 38 In 2016, the U.S. Food and Drug Administration granted approval of the use of SDF for halting caries progression. In 2021, the World Health Organization certified SDF as an essential medicine. 39 Notably, its nonaerosol-generating nature and low transmission risk during the COVID-19 pandemic have made it a preferred choice for cavity control. 37 This may explain why SDF has become the most frequently cited keyword followed by ‘randomized clinical trial’. A recommended treatment strategy involves two visits for managing root caries using SDF. 40 After application of SDF during the first visit, the treated lesion becomes darker and harder, facilitating easier cavity preparation. During the second visit, dentists restore cavities using glass ionomer cement or resin-based materials.

Keyword burst detection is a valuable analytical technique for identifying the keywords that gain increased attention in root caries research. The discovery of cariogenic bacteria led to a surge in laboratory studies. These studies aimed to understand the mechanism of root caries. At the same time, focus increased on the balance between demineralization and remineralization in dental caries. This understanding of root caries aetiology led to new ideas and discoveries about antibacterial materials and drugs. These drugs help inhibit demineralization and promote remineralization. Effective treatments are needed, so clinical studies have grown. These clinical studies assessing the effectiveness of anticaries agents in treating root caries. The goal is to develop evidence-based treatments. The prevention of root caries has gained more attention because of increasing economic burden of dental caries and the need for cost-effective preventive measures. Moreover, researchers are investigating the epidemiology of root caries in different countries and communities. These epidemiological studies enhance future studies, aiming to examine risk factors which are crucial to formulate effective preventive care.

The other keywords listed in Table 2 provide researchers with insights into recent studies on root caries. Systematic reviews are crucial for studying root caries as they offer a comprehensive, unbiased, and reliable evidence source to guide decision-making, identify knowledge gaps, and inform future research. 26 , 41 , 42 , 43 , 44 , 45 , 46 Randomized clinical trials provide high-quality evidence regarding the effectiveness of interventions for root caries and enable comparisons between different approaches. 33 , 47 , 48 Randomized clinical trials help inform evidence-based practice and contribute to improving the prevention and management of root caries. Sodium fluoride is frequently used as a prevention strategy in the studies of root caries due to its ability to promote remineralization, cost-effectiveness, efficacy, and safety. 49 , 50 Analysing the extent and severity of demineralized dentin enables accurate diagnosis and effective treatment of root caries lesions. Additionally, demineralized dentin is commonly used in in vitro studies to evaluate the efficacy of various anticaries agents. 51 , 52 , 53 Root caries is often associated with geriatric dentistry due to the increased susceptibility of older adults to this condition. Geriatric dentists play a crucial role in managing and preventing root caries in older adults through specialized care and interventions tailored to their unique oral health needs. The frequent occurrence of the keyword ‘global burden’ in articles about root caries signifies an increasingly worldwide recognition of the significance and severity associated with untreated root lesions. 54

The references with the most significant citation surges provide valuable insights into current trends and unresolved issues in root caries research. Among the top 10 articles with most citations, three cross-sectional studies and one systematic review explored the risk factors or risk indicators of root caries in specific population. 42 , 55 , 56 , 57 Two systematic reviews concluded different interventions of root caries. 26 , 46 Two reviews emphasizes to study root caries in a periodontal perspective. 58 , 59 The remaining two paper investigated aetiology and efficacy of SDF, respectively. 27 , 28 Understanding the aetiology of root caries is crucial in improving existing treatments. Exposure to the oral environment makes the root surface more susceptible to demineralization with the existence of acid-producing bacteria in the supragingival biofilm. The inorganic concentration and hydroxyapatite crystallites of root dentin and cementum are comparatively lower. It renders them less resistant to acidic environments compared to coronal enamel. Consequently, this accelerates the development and progression of root caries. The most recent article on the aetiology of root cavities was published 7 years ago, 27 highlighting a gap in current research. The involvement of bacteria in the initial stages of organic tooth component degradation remains uncertain. In addition, given the limitations in simulating the actual oral environment, caution is necessary when interpreting study results on the aetiology of root caries.

Academics and researchers use bibliometrics for statistical analyses of journal articles and calculate their cooccurrence counts. They also use bibliometrics analyses to track output and impact of countries, institutions, and researchers. Bibliometric analysis is an efficient method for identifying research trends and frontiers on a specific topic, 15 VOSviewer is one of the most commonly used tools for bibliometric research, creating clear and user-friendly mapping. 60 This tool employs quantitative analysis to create visual representations identifying research clusters, collaboration networks, and keyword cooccurrence patterns. 61 , 62 The comprehensive science mapping process involves data retrieval, preprocessing, network extraction, normalization, mapping, analysis, visualization, and interpretation. WoSCC is a well-established and widely used database that covers a wide range of academic disciplines. It provides access to high-quality, peer-reviewed publications, which ensures the reliability and credibility of the data. However, there are limitations to this review. The study limits its scope to articles indexed in the WoSCC and those written in English literature search. Firstly, using a single database may exclude relevant publications indexed in other databases. Bibliometric analysis can be more comprehensive and have greater generalizability by using multiple databases rather than single database. However, the bibliometric analysis program used in this study for a single database can maintain consistency in data collection. Secondly, including only English publications may introduce bias and fail to represent the global research landscape. Future research could expand on the findings by considering studies in other languages or databases.

## Conclusion

Root caries have gained considerable interest over the past three decades. During the pandemic, the administration of silver diamine fluoride as a noninvasive method has become the focal point of this field.
